Light controls

Moves left → right · c ≈ 300,000 km/s
Violet → blue → green → yellow → red

Press Play to follow the received colour.

Try this: start with green light and compare the received wavelength before and after the source passes. A shorter wavelength is a blueshift; a longer wavelength is a redshift, even if the received colour is not blue or red. At high speeds the received light can shift beyond the visible spectrum. Light always travels at c in this model; motion changes the wavelength and frequency received by the observer.

This model includes special relativity and travel time. The received light was emitted at an earlier source position. With an observer offset from the path, the shift changes smoothly and does not switch exactly when the source passes. In this uniform expansion model, other galaxies recede from every observing location; more distant galaxies recede faster and show greater redshift. Changing the observer changes the distances and measurements, but preserves that relationship. The observer is centred on the screen for convenience, not because it is at the centre of the universe.

The lines above represent a simplified hydrogen spectrum. Astronomers compare a pattern of known spectral lines with the received pattern to measure redshift: z = (received wavelength ÷ emitted wavelength) − 1. Space stretches light during its journey, so its wavelength increases and its frequency decreases. The two wave sketches compare spacing, rather than tracking a single photon.

Model: a uniform, flat universe with constant expansion rate H; a(t) = eHt, v = HD, and 1 + z = a(received) / a(emitted). Here H = 0.08 per model time unit and c = 100 model distance units per time unit. Received light was emitted earlier; the redshift calculation includes this travel history. This is a teaching model, not a reconstruction of the actual galaxies or our universe’s expansion history. The visible window has no physical edge. Local gravity and individual galaxy motions are omitted: nearby galaxies can approach us, and bound stars, galaxies, and galaxy groups do not expand with this grid. NASA: cosmological redshift
